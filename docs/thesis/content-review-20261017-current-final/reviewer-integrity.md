## Overall integrity judgment

The thesis has a coherent, appropriately bounded research spine. It begins with a specific within-station node forecasting problem, states that the outcome is session-reconstructed proxy energy rather than metered power, defines three non-equivalent evaluation protocols, and carries those distinctions through the model comparisons, mechanism diagnostics, interval analyses, and conclusion. The abstract, chapter summaries, and conclusion agree on the central result: MRG-GCN has conditional overall-MAE gains under selected comparisons, but those gains do not reliably extend to nonzero or event targets; the strongest component evidence is conditional on the tested settings; meter validity, event-input arrival time, and operational benefit remain unverified. The manuscript is unusually explicit about post-hoc test reuse and the meaning of its diagnostics, which makes the claims credible within their stated limits.

The central limitation is the evidence domain, not an internal contradiction: Caltech and JPL are two sites in the same ACN data family with overlapping calendar periods, and most supplementary analyses reuse already inspected test periods. The labels remain session-derived energy proxies. The thesis therefore supports controlled, task-specific model comparisons and data-collection priorities, but not future-period/site generalization, metered-power accuracy, online event detection, or operational benefit. The main improvement opportunity is editorial: a large number of caveats, protocol distinctions, and repeated result summaries make the thesis dense. These points are mostly accurate and useful, but can be consolidated without weakening the boundaries.

## Cross-chapter consistency and readability

- **Target and units:** “node proxy load/energy” is repeatedly tied to session-total-energy allocation in kWh/15 min and distinguished from instantaneous or metered power (§§1.1.2, 2.1, 5.4). This is maintained in the abstract and conclusion.
- **Protocol boundaries:** Retrospective Caltech, event-time Caltech, and refitted JPL results are not merged. Their key counts, inputs, splits, and allowed claims are identified in §2.1.1/Table 2-2 and repeated in §3.6/Table 3-1 and §3.8.2. Reuse of previously inspected test periods is disclosed for the later supplements.
- **Event terminology:** `target_event` is an outcome-side post-hoc label; `rule_trigger` is a separate retrospective rule/routing state. The distinction is consistent from §2.4 through Chapters 3–5. One remaining terminology risk is that the behavior graph is encoded as `A^event`/“event relation” while “event” also names target labels and windows. Keep the prose label “behavior co-occurrence” dominant and add a short notation reminder near first use if the equations/tables use “event” shorthand.
- **Contribution/results closure:** Abstract, §3.9, §4.5, §5.5, and conclusion share the same qualified claims. No contradiction was found between overall and subgroup results: the relation increment versus self-loop and the complete model's comparison against TCN answer different questions and are explicitly distinguished.
- **Readability:** The manuscript has clear headings, definitions, table notes, and navigation. It is also very dense: caveats about fixed tests, proxy targets, input timing, and diagnostic scope recur in the abstract, protocol sections, chapter summaries, tables, and conclusion. Consolidating repeated disclaimers and giving each protocol one compact “what this permits” summary would improve flow. Chapter 5 would benefit from a brief transition explaining why directionality, intervals, and the decision interface are the three boundary checks for the same forecasting question.

## Major findings and revisions

### Major strengths

1. The proxy target and its physical interpretation are controlled throughout. The manuscript does not quietly convert session-derived energy into measured power.
2. The central comparisons are carefully bounded. Overall MAE, event/nonzero error, same-parameter gate effects, relation-vs-self-loop effects, and frozen-parameter sensitivity are treated as different claims.
3. Post-hoc reuse and uncertainty are disclosed. The thesis does not relabel an inspected JPL/Caltech test period as independent confirmation.
4. The literature positioning is stronger than a generic “graph forecasting is new” claim: the manuscript identifies precedents for graph, gate, and time-series combinations and locates its contribution in task-specific controls and interpretation.

### Priority revisions

1. **Tighten the chapter-level argument.** Keep one concise protocol-boundary statement in the main text and shorten repeated caveats in chapter summaries and table notes where the same limitation is already explicit. Preserve the distinction among retrospective Caltech, event-time Caltech, and refitted JPL.
2. **Make the innovation claim easy to find.** At the end of §1.4, state the two core contributions in a compact numbered form and map each to one primary result table and one evidence limitation. Keep the exploratory Chapter 5 analyses subordinate to those contributions.
3. **Resolve the remaining “event” shorthand.** At first use of `A^event`, explicitly call it the behavior co-occurrence relation and distinguish it typographically from `target_event` and `rule_trigger`; use that wording consistently in tables and captions.
4. **Make the bin-end assumption operationally prominent.** The release-time convention is mathematically clear, but actual reporting latency is unknown. A single concise callout near the protocol table could prevent readers from treating the event-time task as demonstrated online forecasting.
5. **Keep the practice conclusion at its current level.** The forecast-to-check interface and synthetic LP example are useful as design illustrations; do not infer capacity safety, event protection, or economic benefit until metered and field evidence exists.
6. **Complete end matter before release.** Replace the author-fill placeholders for acknowledgments and research outputs or handle them according to the school template.
